# Review Rubric

Judge each paper or idea on eight axes:

1. Problem significance: does the problem matter beyond a niche benchmark?
2. Topic state: is the problem open, crowded, mature, or insufficiently mapped?
3. Method state: what changes in information, decisions, objective, coordination, interface, or achievable tradeoff relative to the closest method?
4. Mechanism depth: is there a causal chain, a rival explanation, and a discriminating prediction?
5. Technical depth: are the key mechanisms nontrivial and well justified?
6. Evaluation rigor: do the experiments actually support the claims?
7. Communication and provenance: is the paper easy to audit, reproduce, and trust?
8. Risk profile: what missing experiment, assumption, cost, or artifact flaw could sink the paper?

Use reviewer language precisely:

- "Not novel" means a close prior work already contains the key method under materially matching assumptions and claims; topic overlap alone is insufficient.
- "Incremental" means the method delta is real but absorbable by ordinary tuning, a minor heuristic, or routine engineering.
- "Crowded topic" describes competition density, not a rejection verdict.
- "Underspecified" means the method cannot be reproduced or falsified from the text.
- "Under-evaluated" means the paper may be good, but the evidence is insufficient.
- "Overclaimed" means the stated contribution outruns what the data proves.

When giving feedback, separate:

- Fatal issues: likely rejection causes.
- Major issues: substantial but fixable weaknesses.
- Minor issues: polish and clarity gaps.
- Questions: points that could change the recommendation if answered well.
